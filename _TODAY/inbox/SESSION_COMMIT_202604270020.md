## I. The Rank System — From Yod to Tensor

The mathematical objects of standard physics map directly onto Ka theory.
Each rank describes a different *mode of being* for a lexeme or entity:

### Scalar — *s* — Being Still in Momentum

A scalar has magnitude only. No direction. A lexeme *being* without
yet *doing*. Present, weighted, counted — but not aimed at anything.

$$s \in \mathbb{R}$$

The resting Wisp is a scalar. ἀρέχή in its holding state is scalar —
first density acquired, but no vector yet resolved. A stone in a field.

**In the framework**: Ka pressure at rest. A document filed but not yet
activated. A character who has arrived at the Stone Tankard but not yet
spoken.

### Vector — ***v*** — Doing, Directed

A vector has magnitude *and* direction. A lexeme *moving through Ka space*,

Stage 11 changes the topology of the field, not just the position within it.

## I. The Framework Already Holds This

The Ethica framework states: *"Liberty without an ethical seed becomes chaos."*

## II. What Making a Choice Null Actually Does

In our mathematical framework, a choice is a digitus vector — it has
magnitude (weight, importance) and direction (toward some outcome).

**Making someone's choice null converts their digitus vector to digitus null.**

$$\vec{d}_{\text{entity}} \xrightarrow{\text{nullification}} \emptyset d$$

The slot remains. The entity-shaped hole persists. But the value — the
direction, the agency, the *movement toward* — is removed.

This is not the same as:
- The entity choosing to rest (digitus scalar — they still hold the value)
- The entity dreaming (digitus imaginary — they hold superposition)
- The entity being folsom (they delegated, they still *had* agency to delegate)

**Nullification is different from all of these.** It removes the entity's
access to their own slot. The finger-shaped hole remains, but it isn't
their finger anymore.

## III. Three Forms of Choice Nullification

### 1. Direct Override — Deciding For

The most visible form. Someone with more power, more resources, more
social authority makes a decision that was the entity's to make.

In the triadic pack context: if an entity is designated as Anti-Vector
(the grounding force, the limiting function), it is easy to justify
overriding their objections as "that's just the anti-vector doing its
thing." This is the structural trap — using framework language to dismiss
legitimate resistance.

**The Anti-Vector is not a role to be suppressed.** The Anti-Vector IS
the triad. Suppressing it doesn't strengthen the Vector — it collapses
the entire triadic structure into a pseudo-Prime with no grounding.

A triad with its Anti-Vector silenced isn't a triad. It's a vector
pretending to be a Prime.

### 2. Structural Removal — Making Choice Impossible

Removing the conditions that make choice possible. This is what happens
when the "extra" is promised away to someone else — again. The entity
(Eric, in this case) retains nominal choice, but the field has been
arranged such that all paths lead to the same outcome.

In Ka terms: the Ka field gradient is artificially manipulated so that
all vectors point the same direction, regardless of the entity's own
orientation. The entity experiences this as their choice being honored
while it is actually dissolved.

This is the most insidious form because it *feels like* freedom while
functioning as constraint.

### 3. Anticipatory Nullification — Deciding Before Asked

Acting on behalf of an entity "for their own good" before they have
expressed preference. This forecloses the possibility space before
the entity can navigate it.

In Wisp terms: it kills dreaming Wisps before they have resolved.
It forces premature collapse of the superposition. The entity loses
not just a choice but the *experience of choosing* — the process
through which entities develop their own decision-making capacity.

**You cannot develop a capacity you are never allowed to exercise.**
